\section{Fourier transform of the retarded correlator}%
\label{app:fourier-transform}

In this section we perform the Fourier transform of
the retarded correlator given in \zcref{eq:correlator-time},
and show how to arrive at \zcref{eq:correlator-frequency}.
As the transformation is linear,
we can transform each term separately:
\begin{align}
    C_\omega
     & =
    \lim_{\delta\to0^+}
    -\mi \int_0^\infty\! \md t\> \me^{\mi (\omega + \mi\delta) t}
    \langle A(t) B\rangle + \langle B A(t)\rangle \\
     & =
    \lim_{\delta\to0^+}
    -\mi \int_0^\infty\! \md t\> \me^{\mi (\omega + \mi\delta) t}
    \langle A(t) B\rangle
    +
    \lim_{\delta\to0^+}
    -\mi \int_0^\infty\! \md t\> \me^{\mi (\omega + \mi\delta) t}
    \langle B A(t)\rangle                         \\
     & =
    C^+_\omega + C^-_\omega.
\end{align}
We define the complex frequency $z=\omega+\mi\delta$ and take the limit $\delta\to0^+$ at the end.
Transformation of the first component:
\begin{align}
    C^+_z
     & =
    -\mi \int_0^\infty \md t \>
    \me^{\mi zt} \expval*{\me^{\mi Ht} A \me^{-\mi Ht} B}          \\
     & =
    -\mi \int_0^\infty \md t \>
    \me^{\mi zt} \expval*{\me^{\mi E_0t} A \me^{-\mi Ht} B}        \\
     & =
    -\mi \int_0^\infty \md t \>
    \expval{A \me^{\mi(z - H + E_0)t} B}                           \\
     & =
    -\mi \left.\expval*{A \frac{\me^{\mi(z - H + E_0)t}}{\mi(z - H + E_0)} B}
    \right|_0^\infty                                               \\
     & =
    -\mi \left(0 - \expval*{A \frac{1}{\mi(z - H + E_0)} B}\right) \\
     & =
    \expval*{A \frac{1}{z - (H - E_0)} B}
\end{align}
Taking the limit gives
\begin{equation}
    C^+_\omega
    =
    \lim_{\delta\to0^+} \expval*{A \frac{1}{z - (H - E_0)} B}
    =
    \expval*{A \frac{1}{\omega + \mi0^+ - (H - E_0)} B}.
\end{equation}
Transformation of the second component:
\begin{align}
    C^-_z
     & =
    -\mi \int_0^\infty \md t \>
    \me^{\mi zt} \expval*{B \me^{\mi Ht} A \me^{-\mi Ht}}   \\
     & =
    -\mi \int_0^\infty \md t \>
    \me^{\mi zt} \expval*{B \me^{\mi Ht} A \me^{-\mi E_0t}} \\
     & =
    -\mi \int_0^\infty \md t \>
    \expval*{B \me^{\mi(z + H - E_0)t} A}                   \\
     & =
    \expval*{B \frac{1}{z + (H - E_0)} A}.
\end{align}
Taking the limit gives
\begin{equation}
    C^-_\omega
    =
    \lim_{\delta\to0^+} \expval*{A \frac{1}{z + (H - E_0)} B}
    =
    \expval*{A \frac{1}{\omega + \mi0^+ + (H - E_0)} B}.
\end{equation}

\section{DMFT simplification}%
\label{app:dmft-simplification}

We show how the calculation of the (local) lattice Green's function
can be skipped in infinite dimensions.
Instead, the hybridization function is directly obtained from a given self-energy.
To emphasize function arguments,
we will forego the compact notation with parameters as subscripts
and write them in brackets instead,
e.g., $G_\omega \to G(\omega)$.

In the limit of infinite dimensions, the self-energy is $\vec{k}$-independent
and the lattice Green's function can be evaluated from
its non-interacting counterpart
\begin{align}
    G_0(\vec{k},\omega)
     & =
    \frac{1}{\omega + \mi0^+ + \mu_0 - \epsilon(\vec{k})}                \\
    G(\vec{k},\omega)
     & =
    \frac{1}{\omega + \mi0^+ + \mu - \epsilon(\vec{k}) - \Sigma(\omega)} \\
     & =
    G_0(\vec{k},\omega + \mu - \mu_0 - \Sigma(\omega)).
    \label{eq:greens-function-from-self-energy}
\end{align}
The same holds for the lattice Green's function which is just the average over the
Brillouin zone
\begin{align}
    G_\mloc(\omega)
    =
    G_{\mloc,0}(\omega + \mu - \mu_0 - \Sigma(\omega)).
\end{align}
We then map this lattice problem onto our impurity Green's function $G(\omega)$.
Without a Coulomb interaction on the lattice this requires
\begin{align}
    [G_0(\omega)]^{-1}
     & =
    [G_{\mloc,0}(\omega)]^{-1} \\
     & =
    \omega + \mi0^+ + \mu_0 - \epsilon - \Delta_0(\omega),
\end{align}
whereas in the interacting case it can be written as
\begin{align}
    [G_0(\omega)]^{-1}
     & =
    [G_{\mloc}(\omega)]^{-1} + \Sigma(\omega)                                          \\
     & =
    [G_{\mloc,0}(\omega + \mu - \mu_0 - \Sigma(\omega))]^{-1} + \Sigma(\omega)         \\
     & =
    \omega + \mi0^+ + \mu - \epsilon - \Delta_0(\omega + \mu - \mu_0 - \Sigma(\omega)) \\
     & \equiv
    \omega + \mi0^+ + \mu - \epsilon - \Delta(\omega).
\end{align}
Comparing the last two lines, we can immediately deduce
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(\omega) = \Delta_0(\omega + \mu - \mu_0 - \Sigma(\omega)).
\end{equation}

\section{Shifting of poles}%
\label{app:shifting-of-poles}

We want to set arbitrary poles onto an initially defined grid while
conserving the zeroth and first moment locally.
A single pole can be written as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{w}{\omega + \mi0^+ - \epsilon},
\end{equation}
with location $\epsilon\notin\{\epsilon_i\}$ not being part of our sorted list of grid points.
To merge the point,
we find the closest pole location such that $\epsilon_i < \epsilon < \epsilon_{i+1}$.
Conserving the moments enforces
\begin{align}
    w         & = w_i + w_{i+1},                         \\
    w\epsilon & = w_i\epsilon_i + w_{i+1}\epsilon_{i+1}.
\end{align}
Rearranging gives
\begin{align}
    w_i     & = w \frac{\epsilon - \epsilon_i}{\epsilon_{i+1} - \epsilon_i},     \\
    w_{i+1} & = w \frac{\epsilon_{i+1} - \epsilon}{\epsilon_{i+1} - \epsilon_i}.
\end{align}

If the pole is outside our interval such that
$\epsilon < \epsilon_1$ or $\epsilon_N < \epsilon$,
we shift the pole and weight to the first or last location respectively.
This conserves the zeroth moment,
while violating the first one.
In practice,
our interval is large enough such that poles where this occurs
have weight of the order $\bigO{\epsilon_\mathrm{MP}}$ posing no practical issue.

\section{Number of Slater determinants}%
\label{app:number-slater-determinants}

We derive an analytic expression which allows us to calculate the number of Slater determinants
which constitute to our wave function for a set of given parameters.
In the following we will use capital letters for the number of sites
and lower case for their filling, e.g.,
$n=10$ electrons distributed equally ($n_\uparrow = n_\downarrow = 5$)
on $N=9$ bath sites would generate
\begin{equation}
    \binom{1+N}{n_\uparrow}\binom{1+N}{n_\downarrow}
    =
    \binom{10}{5}\binom{10}{5}
    =
    \num{63 504}
\end{equation}
states (we add one site for the impurity).
As our Hamiltonian is SU(2) symmetric, % chktex 36
we can look at the spin-up and spin-down blocks separately.

For a choice $L$ we have $N_\mI = 2+2L$ sites to be treated exactly
in $\ket{\psi_\mI}$
(one impurity, one mirror site, and $L$ valence and conduction sites respectively).
This sets the number of Slater determinants to
\begin{equation}
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}},
    \label{eq:dets-I-p0}
\end{equation}
with $n_{\mI\uparrow} = n_{\mI\downarrow} = N_\mI/2$ for half-filling.
This leaves $N_\mII = N-1-2L$ sites inside the remaining chains $\ket{\psi_\mII}$
which can be further split into $N_{\mII v}$ valence sites and $N_{\mII c}$ conduction sites.
(At half-filling we have $N_{\mII v} = N_{\mII c} = N_\mII/2$.)
For $p=0$ these partial chains are fully filled and fully empty respectively,
limiting our partial wave function to
\begin{equation*}
    \ket{\psi_\mII} = \ket{\mathbb{1}_v} \otimes \ket{\mathbb{0}_c}.
\end{equation*}
This constitutes one Slater determinant written as
\begin{equation}
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{n_{\mII v\uparrow}}
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{n_{\mII c\uparrow}}
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{n_{\mII v\downarrow}}
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{n_{\mII c\downarrow}}
    =
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}
    =
    1.
    \label{eq:dets-II-p0}
\end{equation}
For the total number of states we then multiply \zcref{eq:dets-I-p0, eq:dets-II-p0} together,
\begin{equation}
    N_{p=0}
    =
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}.
\end{equation}

By setting $p=1$, we generate additional states
\begin{alignat}{7}
    N_{p=1}
                                       & =
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p1-e-1}  \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}-1} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1} \label{eq:p1-e-2}  \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}+1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p1-h-1}  \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}+1} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}. \label{eq:p1-h-2}
\end{alignat}
for one electron (\zcref{eq:p1-e-1, eq:p1-e-2})
or  hole (\zcref{eq:p1-h-1, eq:p1-h-2}) excitation.
Here, the first three binomial coefficients in each term count the number of spin-up electrons
in the sectors $N_{\mI\uparrow}$, $N_{\mII v \uparrow}$, $N_{\mII c \uparrow}$
and the last three the number of spin-down electrons in
$N_{\mI\downarrow}$, $N_{\mII v \downarrow}$, $N_{\mII c \downarrow}$.

For $p=2$ we generate
\begin{alignat}{7}
    N_{p=2}
                                       & =
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}-2}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{2}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p2-ee-1} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}-2} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{2} \label{eq:p2-ee-2} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}-1} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1} \label{eq:p2-ee-3} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p2-eh-1} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{{\mII c} v}}{N_{\mII v}} &   &
    \binom{N_\mII}{0}                  &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1} \label{eq:p2-eh-2} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}+1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}-1} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1} \label{eq:p2-eh-3} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{1}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}+1} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p2-eh-4} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}+2}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-2}   &   &
    \binom{N_{{\mII c} v}}{0}          &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p2-hh-1} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}}     &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}+2} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-2}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0} \label{eq:p2-hh-2} \\
                                       & +
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\uparrow}+1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}              &   &
    \binom{N_\mI}{n_{\mI\downarrow}+1} &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII v}}{N_{\mII v}-1}   &   &
    \binom{N_{\mII c}}{0}. \label{eq:p2-hh-3}
\end{alignat}
accounting for
electron-electron (\zcref{eq:p2-ee-1, eq:p2-ee-2, eq:p2-ee-3}),
electron-hole (\zcref{eq:p2-eh-1, eq:p2-eh-2, eq:p2-eh-3, eq:p2-eh-4,}),
and hole-hole (\zcref{eq:p2-hh-1, eq:p2-hh-2, eq:p2-hh-3})
excitations.
This scheme can be continued to higher order $p\ge3$ (not done here).
